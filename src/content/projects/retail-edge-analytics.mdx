## Context

Store operators wanted to know how many people came in, how they moved, and how trial rooms, billing counters and greeting zones were used, measured from the store's own camera feeds. Retail Edge Analytics computes six KPIs (key performance indicators) on a Raspberry Pi 5 inside each store. The system started as 40+ pilot deployments, is live in 400+ stores, and has a 700-store rollout under contract.

The six KPIs are footfall entry and exit, passerby counts, trial-room usage, billing-counter activity, greeting-zone activity, and movement heatmaps. A cron job syncs store sales data so that footfall can be turned into conversion metrics.

## Constraints

Each store runs the whole pipeline on one Raspberry Pi 5, an ARM64 board with four CPU cores, reading several RTSP (Real-Time Streaming Protocol) camera feeds at once. The models had to be small and quantised, and the final deployment runs without the PyTorch runtime, so every pre- and post-processing step had to work outside the training framework.

Store cameras see backlit doorways, people on the far side of glass shopfronts, people lingering at the threshold, and staff walking through the same zones as customers. Each of those situations produced wrong counts until the pipeline had a specific rule for it.

With 400+ devices in the field, the data on each device has to survive a device failure, and the connectivity of every device has to be visible centrally.

Detection and classification run once per RTSP feed, and every KPI on that camera reuses the results. Movement heatmaps add to each cell of a normalised grid at most once per track.

The data and operations layer stores KPIs in daily partitioned SQLite databases with retention, replicated continuously to AWS S3 by Litestream. Host-level connectivity heartbeats feed fleet monitoring. Store staff draw camera zones in a browser zone-annotation tool and read results in a React dashboard.

## Hard problems

### The NCNN port that turned every box into one pixel

After the move to NCNN, the detector received the wrong input tensor, and every detected box decoded to roughly one pixel. Nothing raised an error. The pipeline kept running while three KPIs stopped producing meaningful values.

The fix was to rebuild the decode path and verify box geometry against FP32 reference outputs, which restored parity with the FP32 baseline.

### Periodic 2× latency spikes on a four-core Pi

The processing loop showed periodic spikes to twice its normal latency. Two architectural changes removed them. Detection and classification now run once per RTSP feed, with results shared across every KPI on that camera, and the Pi's four cores are partitioned into dedicated NCNN pipelines.

With both changes in place, the Pi holds 90 ms median inference per camera without the periodic spikes.

### Footfall counts that trusted a single line

Naive line-crossing counted people seen through the glass shopfront and people lingering at the threshold as entries. The replacement uses an inner-boundary rule derived from each camera's zone config, gate buffers sized relative to frame resolution, and union-find clustering of tracks into groups.

Footfall counts now reach 94% agreement with manual counts.

### A demographic head that predicted 39% male against 75% actual

Manual audits of 451 people across five observation windows showed a systematic bias: the raw demographic head predicted 39% male where the audited share was 75%. Prior-shift reweighting corrects the classifier's output probabilities for the difference between training-set class priors and the priors observed in stores.

### Excluding staff without losing customers

Majority voting over noisy per-frame customer/employee predictions discarded about 18% of genuine entrants as employees. Employee exclusion now requires sustained high-confidence employee evidence across a track before a person is removed from customer counts.

A related threshold change lowered detector confidence from 0.45 to 0.30, after validation on store footage showed that the lower threshold recovered backlit doorway detections.
