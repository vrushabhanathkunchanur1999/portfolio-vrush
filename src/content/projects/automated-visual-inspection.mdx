## Context

An electronics manufacturer needed every unit on its line checked for correct component placement, visible defects and seal quality, with a pass/fail verdict per unit. Automated Visual Inspection runs on 4 stations, covers three product variants, inspects 1,400+ units per day, and has been in production for 1 year.

Each inspection returns a pass/fail verdict together with an annotated image of the unit.

## Constraints

Three product variants share one inspection service, and each variant is routed to its own pipeline. On the plant floor, lighting, part alignment and false rejects all had to be resolved during commissioning.

Training data came from production: 2,500 annotated images across 6 component and defect classes for detection, and 9,000 normal against 1,500 anomalous rim patches for the seal classifier. Anomalous patches make up about one in seven of the seal training set.

## What I built

The inference service is a GPU-backed Flask application, containerised with Docker on CUDA 12.8. The service routes each of the three product variants to a dedicated pipeline, loads all models once at startup, and holds median latency at 1,200 ms per unit.

Component and defect detection uses YOLOX-S and YOLOX-M detectors trained on 2,500 annotated production images across 6 classes, reaching 0.96 mAP@0.5 (mean average precision at 0.5 intersection over union).

The seal pipeline in OpenCV runs these stages in order:

1. **Mean-shift colour quantisation** to reduce the image to a small set of colours.
2. **HSV segmentation**, separating the seal by hue, saturation and value.
3. **Homography alignment** of the part to a fixed reference frame.
4. **Batched ONNX classification** of 36 rim ROIs (regions of interest) with the seal-anomaly classifier. ONNX is the Open Neural Network Exchange model format.
5. **Consecutive-failure aggregation**, which judges the seal on runs of consecutive failing ROIs around the rim rather than on any single ROI.

The seal-anomaly classifier reaches 94% precision and 96% recall at the deployed threshold.

## Hard problems

### A false pass caused by ROIs landing on background

Misalignment could place the rim ROIs on background instead of on the seal. The classifier then saw no seal defect in those ROIs, and the unit passed. The failure mode was systematic rather than random.

The fix added pixel-ratio and ROI-coverage gates, which reject ROIs that do not sit on the seal, plus a geometric seating check built on connected components, min-area-rect fitting and a distance transform. All thresholds were calibrated on labelled OK and NOK (not OK) samples. Together, the gates and the seating check eliminated the false-pass mode.

### Commissioning four stations on the plant floor

All four stations were commissioned on site across phased deployments. Lighting, alignment and false-reject issues were resolved directly with the plant floor manager.

Field failures from commissioning were converted into new validation samples, so problems found on the floor became validation data.
